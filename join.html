Lab members answer a few questions about life at THRIVE

TL

Tessa Lapelyte

Research Project Student

Research placementFinal-year Psychology
What is your role in the lab, and what are you currently working on?

I was a research placement student; currently, I am in my final year of my psychology degree.

What drew you to the THRIVE Lab, and what has surprised you most since joining?

I was really interested in Faith's research and thought I could learn a lot from her. I was surprised by how many people are involved in the THRIVE lab; it was a lovely surprise to have a community where we could share ideas and project progress.

How would you describe the lab culture, and is there a favourite moment or tradition?

Everyone is lovely and accepting; I never felt like I would be judged for anything. I like the idea of sharing both our achievements and losses at the start of our THRIVE meetings, as it shows you are not alone in your successes or struggles.

How has working with Faith and the team shaped your skills, thinking, or career plans?

Working with Faith has definitely improved my confidence in general and made me realise I want to continue to be involved in the THRIVE lab in any way possible moving forward.

What advice would you give someone thinking of joining the lab?

Best advice would be to get familiarised with the previous research projects of Faith or the people from the lab, as it will show what the THRIVE lab is about and if any of those projects sound interesting, I'm sure they would fit right in.

YL

Yasmin Lavictoire

PhD Student & Doctoral Tutor in Psychology

PhD researcherMenopause & sleep
What is your role in the lab, and what are you currently working on?

I am a PhD researcher investigating the relationship between menopausal sleep disturbance and positive psychological traits, with the aim of developing a positive psychological intervention to support sleep during this life stage. Currently, I am working on a scoping review to understand the research landscape and inform my future studies.

What drew you to the THRIVE Lab, and what has surprised you most since joining?

I was drawn to Faith’s expertise in digital research methodology, which aligned with my interests. What surprised me most was the diversity of members’ research backgrounds and interests, while we all share a common goal: to better understand and support mental and physical health through digital technology.

How would you describe the lab culture, and is there a favourite moment or tradition?

The lab provides a welcoming, inclusive and supportive environment, where all lab members collaborate to generate insightful research. Lab meetings have been a particular highlight: it has been inspiring to hear about others’ research and findings, connect with other researchers, and explore ideas and practices to adopt in my own research.

How has working with Faith and the team shaped your skills, thinking, or career plans?

I have adored working with Faith: her exceptional supervision, support and expertise have equipped me with the skills and opportunities to grow as a researcher. Her guidance has encouraged me to think critically about my research protocols and methodology, and I have carried this learning forward into shaping my PhD research. Equally, collaborating with other members of the lab, such as Bex, has developed my knowledge of how to run an experience sampling study, develop study protocols and navigate the challenges associated with this methodology.

What advice would you give someone thinking of joining the lab?

Be prepared to learn from people with a wide range of interests and expertise, and don’t be afraid to ask questions or share your ideas. There is so much to gain from the support and collaboration within the lab, so make the most of opportunities to get involved and learn from one another.

Applying for a PhD: How It Actually Works

Three steps. Click a card to read more about it.

1. Reaching out to the supervisor

Send your CV, your research interests and eventually a couple of project ideas that connect to the lab's work. Having no ideas yet is perfectly fine: we will usually propose some directions and refine them with you. It also helps enormously to arrive having already searched and selected potential funding schemes you want to target, so we can shape the project around what you are actually eligible for.

Email Faith

2. Then find the money

This is the genuinely difficult part. Four profiles cover most cases:

  • you and the supervisor build a project and apply for a scholarship together;
  • the supervisor already holds a grant for a specific project and recruits for it;
  • you arrive with a scholarship already secured; or
  • you self-fund, which we do not recommend unless you are one of the lucky few with money to spare.

Funding opportunities are listed below, but you should also search for others.

3. Then apply

With a target in mind, gather the documents and write the proposal. Fingers crossed!

PhD Funding Opportunities

Funding is the usual barrier between a plan and a PhD. Below are some opportunities, but you should also search for others.

★★★2027 entry · apply to Sussex by 1 December 2026

SEDarc (ESRC) scholarships

The ESRC's doctoral training partnership for the South East, and the closest fit for most of what this lab does: Psychology is one of its eligible subject areas. Home-rate fees and a UKRI stipend over 3.5 years, or 1+3.5 if you do not have a Masters yet, plus a funded three-month placement outside academia. The project has to sit inside one of the five SEDarc themes, and a proposal claiming the advanced quantitative methods or data skills steer is what this lab is built for.

You apply to Sussex by 1 December 2026, complete with a supervisor statement; Sussex nominates to the consortium at the end of January. Write to Faith well before November.

  • Fees + stipend
  • 3.5 or 1+3.5 years
  • 3-month funded placement
