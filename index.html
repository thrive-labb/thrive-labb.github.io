Recent Update European Association of Developmental Psychology (EADP)

The bidirectional relationship between physical pain and anxiety and depression in young people

Hai Tran, University of Sussex, UK

This August, THRIVE Doctoral Researcher Hai Tran took part in the 2026 International Summer School, jointly organised by the European Association of Developmental Psychology (EADP), the European Association for Research on Adolescence (EARA), and the Society for Research on Adolescence (SRA).

Held from 14–18 August at the 50|50 Hotel Belmont in De Veluwe National Park, the Netherlands, the Summer School brought together 24 doctoral researchers from around the world for an intensive programme of research presentations, feedback sessions, and discussions with leading scholars in adolescent development.

During the event, Hai presented her PhD research on the bidirectional relationships between physical pain and anxiety and depression in young people. The opportunity to receive detailed feedback from both peers and established researchers provided valuable insights that will help inform the development of the project and future research directions.

Reflecting on the experience, Hai highlighted the benefits of engaging with an international community of early career researchers and experts in the field. The Summer School offered a unique opportunity to exchange ideas, build new collaborations, and immerse themselves in a lively and supportive research environment.

The experience was both intellectually stimulating and personally rewarding, leaving Hai inspired and energised for the next stages of their doctoral research.

Feasibility and Acceptability of Ecological Momentary Assessment (EMA) for Chronic Pain: Implications for Adaptive mHealth Interventions

Harding RP et al.

Presenter: Bex Harding
Event: Society for Ambulatory Assessment Conference 2026
Venue: Conference Programme (see link)
Series: Conference Presentation

This presentation examines the feasibility and acceptability of Ecological Momentary Assessment (EMA) for individuals living with chronic pain using a 14-day intensive data collection protocol.

High retention and response rates support the use of EMA to capture real-time pain, mood, sleep, and self-management behaviours, providing a foundation for developing personalised and adaptive mHealth interventions for chronic pain management.

Bidirectional associations between pain, anxiety, and depressive symptoms during adolescence: Findings from the Quebec Newborn Twin Study

Poster Presentation | SSM Annual Conference 2026

Dates: September 9th - 11th, 2026
Format: Poster Presentation
Study: Quebec Newborn Twin Study
Key Focus: Sex differences (male/female)
Event: SSM Annual Conference 2026

PhD student Hai Tran investigates the bidirectional associations between pain and internalising problems (anxiety and depressive symptoms) across adolescence, using both phenotypic and genetically sensitive cross-lagged panel models. The research draws on data from the Quebec Newborn Twin Study and tests whether the associations are moderated by birth sex (male, female).
