Exploring the feasibility of real-time assessment for chronic pain

Rebecca Harding, University of Sussex, UK

THRIVE Doctoral Researcher Rebecca Harding recently presented her latest research at the Society for Ambulatory Assessment (SAA) Conference in Vienna, Austria.

The study investigated the feasibility and acceptability of Ecological Momentary Assessment (EMA) for people living with chronic pain. EMA uses brief smartphone-based assessments completed throughout the day to capture experiences in real time, providing valuable insights into fluctuations in pain, mood, sleep, and self-management behaviours.

Participants completed up to eight assessments per day over a two-week period. Findings showed high levels of engagement and retention, demonstrating that intensive real-time data collection is both feasible and acceptable for people living with chronic pain.

The research provides an important foundation for the development of adaptive mobile health interventions that can deliver more personalised and responsive support to individuals managing chronic pain.

Bidirectional associations between pain, anxiety, and depressive symptoms during adolescence: Findings from the Quebec Newborn Twin Study

Poster Presentation | SSM Annual Conference 2026

Dates: September 9th - 11th, 2026
Format: Poster Presentation
Study: Quebec Newborn Twin Study
Key Focus: Sex differences (male/female)
Event: SSM Annual Conference 2026

PhD student Hai Tran investigates the bidirectional associations between pain and internalising problems (anxiety and depressive symptoms) across adolescence, using both phenotypic and genetically sensitive cross-lagged panel models. The research draws on data from the Quebec Newborn Twin Study and tests whether the associations are moderated by birth sex (male, female).

AI and Digital Innovations in Addiction Treatment

SSA Annual Conference 2026 Plenary Session

Speaker: Dr Faith Matcham
Event: SSA Annual Conference 2026
Session Type: Plenary Panel Discussion
Theme: AI and Digital Health Innovation

This plenary session will explore how artificial intelligence and emerging digital technologies can transform addiction research, prevention, and treatment. The discussion will focus on the potential of these innovations to enable earlier detection of risk, deliver more personalised interventions, and improve outcomes for people experiencing addiction.

Bringing together four leaders in health system transformation, clinical AI, mobile health, and digital mental health, the session will examine current opportunities, challenges, and future directions for integrating digital technologies into addiction care and research.
