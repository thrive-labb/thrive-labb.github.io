SOCIETY FOR SOCIAL MEDICINE (SSM) ANNUAL CONFERENCE

Pain and Internalising Problems During Adolescence: Findings from the Quebec Newborn Twin Study

Poster Presentation | SSM Annual Conference 2026

Format: Poster Presentation
Study: Quebec Newborn Twin Study
Participants: 712 Twin Pairs
Focus: Pain, Anxiety and Depression in Adolescence

THRIVE Doctoral Researcher Hai Tran will present her latest research at the Society for Social Medicine (SSM) Annual Conference 2026. The study examines the longitudinal relationships between pain frequency and internalising problems, including anxiety and depressive symptoms, during adolescence.

Using data from 712 twin pairs participating in the Quebec Newborn Twin Study, the research investigated associations between pain, anxiety, and depression at ages 12, 13, and 14. Both Random-Intercept Cross-Lagged Panel Models (RI-CLPM) and Monozygotic Twin Difference Cross-Lagged Panel Models (MZD-CLPM) were used to better understand how these experiences influence one another over time while accounting for potential genetic and environmental confounding factors.

The findings revealed strong continuity in pain frequency, anxiety, and depressive symptoms across adolescence. However, contrary to expectations, there was little evidence that pain and internalising problems directly influenced one another over time. Instead, elevated anxiety and depressive symptoms at age 13 were associated with an increased likelihood of experiencing frequent pain at age 14, whereas pain itself did not significantly predict later mental health difficulties.

These results suggest that the relationship between pain and internalising problems may be age-dependent, with associations becoming more apparent during mid-adolescence. The findings highlight the importance of supporting adolescents experiencing anxiety and depression, as improving mental health may help reduce the risk of later physical health problems, including persistent pain.

The study also demonstrates the value of genetically informed longitudinal methods when investigating developmental pathways linking physical and mental health. Differences observed between the two modelling approaches highlight the importance of carefully considering potential sources of confounding when interpreting longitudinal associations.

SOCIETY FOR SOCIAL MEDICINE (SSM) ANNUAL CONFERENCE

Bidirectional associations between pain, anxiety, and depressive symptoms during adolescence: Findings from the Quebec Newborn Twin Study

Poster Presentation | SSM Annual Conference 2026

Dates: September 9th - 11th, 2026
Format: Poster Presentation
Study: Quebec Newborn Twin Study
Key Focus: Sex differences (male/female)
Event: SSM Annual Conference 2026

PhD student Hai Tran investigates the bidirectional associations between pain and internalising problems (anxiety and depressive symptoms) across adolescence, using both phenotypic and genetically sensitive cross-lagged panel models. The research draws on data from the Quebec Newborn Twin Study and tests whether the associations are moderated by birth sex (male, female).

AI and Digital Innovations in Addiction Treatment

SSA Annual Conference 2026 Plenary Session

Speaker: Dr Faith Matcham
Event: SSA Annual Conference 2026
Session Type: Plenary Panel Discussion
Theme: AI and Digital Health Innovation

This plenary session will explore how artificial intelligence and emerging digital technologies can transform addiction research, prevention, and treatment. The discussion will focus on the potential of these innovations to enable earlier detection of risk, deliver more personalised interventions, and improve outcomes for people experiencing addiction.

Bringing together four leaders in health system transformation, clinical AI, mobile health, and digital mental health, the session will examine current opportunities, challenges, and future directions for integrating digital technologies into addiction care and research.
